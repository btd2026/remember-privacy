Remember — Privacy policy

Effective October 5, 2026. Remember is in a private beta run by one developer, Faith-Coded Technologies.

Remember is a voice-first journal. What you write is yours. This page says exactly where it goes.

Only on your phone

  • Turning your voice into text. Speech recognition runs on your iPhone.
  • Your search index, and everything you type into Search.
  • The photos of journal pages you take, after they have been read.

Sent to the Remember server

So your journal survives a lost phone, each entry is sent to a server run by the developer: its text, its lines, its recordings (unless you turn off Keep original audio), and the takeaways and index card made from it.

  • Everything is encrypted on the server's disk, with a key kept apart from the data, so a copy of the database or a backup cannot be read.
  • The running server can read your entries, in order to sync them and to send them to an AI model as described below.
  • The server is backed up each night, encrypted, to the developer's iCloud Drive. The newest 30 backups are kept.
  • You sign in with an invite code. Your phone keeps its own sign-in token in the iOS Keychain.

Sent to an AI model

After you save an entry, its text is sent to an AI model so Remember can write takeaways, index it, and find related entries and Scripture. When you ask Remember to read photographed pages, the photos are sent once for that reading and are not stored on the server.

Requests go through OpenRouter and are routed only to providers that do not retain the data or train on it (zero data retention).

What is never collected

  • No advertising, no analytics, no tracking, and no third-party SDKs in the app.
  • Server logs record counts, timings and versions, never the words of your entries.
  • Your journal is never sold or shared with anyone else.

Deleting

Deleting an entry in the app removes it from your journal on every device. To have everything you have written erased from the server, email the address below; it will be done within 30 days, by which time the last backup holding it has also expired.
