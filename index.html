Remember — Privacy policy

Effective November 2, 2026. Operated by Brian Di Bassinga.

Remember is a private journal. You speak or type an entry, and Remember helps you look back over it and see how it connects to what you wrote before and to Scripture. What you write is yours. This page says exactly where it goes.

Only on your phone

  • Turning your voice into text. Speech recognition runs on your iPhone. Recording, transcribing, checking your lines and saving all work offline.
  • Search. Your journal's search index and the passages Remember finds for you stay on the phone. The phone finds the candidates, and only the ones described below leave it.
  • Drafts and caches. Unsaved drafts, local indexes and cached journal material are kept in your phone's protected storage, separately for each account.
  • Photographed pages. Reading a photographed journal page is not available in this release.

Sent to the Remember server

To keep your journal safe and in sync, the app sends each saved entry to a server run by the developer: its text, its lines, the recording (unless you turn off Keep original audio) and the takeaways, index cards, themes and connections made for it.

  • Where it lives. The server runs on Fly.io in the United States.
  • Encryption. Journal text, recordings and other content fields are encrypted at rest, with a key kept apart from the data. Some operational details, such as record identifiers, dates and counts, are not encrypted. This is server-side encryption: the running service can read your entries, so that it can sync them and, with your permission, send them to an AI model as described below.
  • Backups. The server is backed up nightly to the developer's own storage. Content fields stay encrypted in those copies. The newest 30 backups are kept, and older ones are deleted.
  • Signing in. You sign in with Apple or Google through Supabase Auth, which handles identity and sessions, including the email address or Apple private relay address the provider shares. A small number of accounts created by the developer, such as the account given to App Review, sign in with an email address and password through the same service. Anyone can create an account with Apple or Google. Nobody can create an email-and-password account from the app. Sign-in credentials are kept in the iOS Keychain. Your journal, recordings and AI processing stay on the Remember server, not with Supabase.
  • Ownership. Remember never treats a matching email address or name as proof that an older journal is yours. Entries already on your phone are added to a signed-in account only after you preview and confirm them.
  • Optional answers. When you sign in for the first time, you can tell us your first name, an email address for feedback, how you journal now and what you hope Remember helps with. Every answer is optional, encrypted like your entries, and seen only by the developer.

Sent to an AI model, only with your permission

Some features use an AI model: takeaways, the index card for each entry, themes and Bible verses for an entry, connections between your entries, Look back summaries, and answers to questions you ask in Explore. The app asks your permission before any of them run. The choice is the “Find connections with AI” switch in Settings. If you leave it off, you can still record, write, read, search and sync your whole journal.

When it's on, here is what the AI model receives:

  • After you save an entry, or when you import or reconnect earlier entries: that entry's text and lines, and short passages from earlier entries your phone picked as possibly related.
  • When you ask a question in Explore: your question, up to your last three questions in that conversation, and short passages from the entries your phone found. For a broad question, it also receives each candidate entry's date, one-line summary, tags and first 400 characters, so it can pick the ones that answer it. Remember's server does not store your questions or the answers.
  • When you ask for a Look back summary: lines from the entries in that period.

AI requests go from Remember's server through OpenRouter to the model provider. Remember uses only zero-data-retention routes, which forbid the provider from keeping what is sent or training on it. The results, such as takeaways, cards and connections, are stored encrypted on Remember's server as part of your journal. Turning the switch off stops new processing. Results already made can stay in your journal until you delete them or your account. AI output can be wrong, so Remember always points back to your own entries so that you can check it.

Optional product insights

Product measurement is off until you turn it on. If you opt in, Remember records which screens and actions you use from a fixed list: whether an action succeeded or failed, rough durations, the day (UTC), and the app version and build. A pseudonymous identifier counts returning use. It isn't your name, but it isn't anonymous either.

  • These events never include your journal's words, titles, Explore questions, notes, entry identifiers, photos, audio, screenshots, screen recordings, names or email addresses. Use of the crisis resources is never recorded against your account.
  • Events go to Remember's own server, after any unsaved capture has finished. Remember uses no advertising identifier and no third-party analytics service.
  • Raw events are deleted after 30 days. Turning measurement off stops collection, clears queued events and removes your stored events. Deleting your account removes them too.

Feedback you send

If you tap Send feedback after taking a screenshot, Remember opens your Mail app with a message to the developer. Nothing is sent until you press Send in Mail. The message contains what you type and your app and iOS versions, and the screenshot only if you choose to attach it.

What Remember never does

  • No advertising, no advertising identifiers, no tracking across apps or websites, and no session replay.
  • Your journal, and anything made from it, is never sold, shared with advertisers, or given to anyone else. Not even in summary or combined form.
  • Server logs record counts, timings and versions, never the words of your entries.
  • Remember does not read your entries to contact you or anyone else. It shows crisis resources in Settings and after a save, and never reports to anyone.

Your choices and rights

  • See and export: Settings → Account lets you export your journal and its recordings at any time.
  • Correct or remove: you can edit or delete any entry or line, and keep or dismiss any connection Remember suggests.
  • Turn off: AI processing and product measurement each have their own switch.
  • Delete your account: Settings → Account → Delete account. This deletes your journal on the server, your recordings, everything made from them, your learning data and your product events, and ends Remember's access to your sign-in. If Remember can't revoke your Sign in with Apple access automatically, it still deletes your data and tells you how to remove that access yourself. The app says what, if anything, stays on your phone.
  • Backups: backups made before you delete your account aren't edited. They're deleted as newer ones replace them, and Remember applies your deletion to any restored backup before serving its data again.
  • For anything else, including a copy of your data or a question about it, write to the contact below.

Children

Remember is not intended for children under 13, and does not knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child under 13 has an account, write to us and we will delete it.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted here with a new effective date.
